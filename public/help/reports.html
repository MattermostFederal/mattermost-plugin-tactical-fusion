Aviation Reports

A METAR, SPECI, TAF or NOTAM pasted into a message is decoded into plain language: a link inside a message, a table when the report is the whole message, a card when it is fenced. Nothing is fetched.

Settings: EnableAvReport, one switch per kind, and EnableAvReportCard for the card. To see what your own server does, run /tactical-fusion examples. Every example below is one of that command's own rows.

What is read

Air operations channels paste weather and notices exactly as the wire carries them, and a reader who does not decode 07012G18KT in their head has to leave the channel to find out what it said. This decorator reads four kinds of report and renders each group in words, beside the report as it was written.

KindRecognized byExample
METAR and SPECI The keyword, or a station and a time group followed by a wind group METAR PHNL 221651Z 07012G18KT 10SM FEW025 SCT045 27/19 A3010
TAF The keyword, a station, a time group and a validity period TAF PGUA 221720Z 2218/2324 07012KT P6SM SCT025
NOTAM, FAA domestic form An exclamation mark, the accountability location, the number and the affected location !HNL 09/123 HNL RWY 08L/26R CLSD 2609221200-2609232359
NOTAM, ICAO form The series and number header, then the Q) to G) fields A1234/26 NOTAMN, on several lines

Nothing is fetched. There is no weather source, no NOTAM feed and no outbound request of any kind. The plugin decodes what somebody pasted, which is what makes it usable on a network with no route to the internet, and it means a report is only as current as the person who pasted it.

A report on one line becomes a link

A report that fits on one line is rewritten into a link, exactly as a date-time group is. The link's label is the report as you wrote it. Hover it and the card gives the station and a one-line summary; click it and the panel shows every decoded group.

The link carries the report text and the instant its time group resolved to, and nothing else. The panel and the page decode the text again on every open and refuse a link whose instant does not reproduce the report's own day, hour and minute, so a link edited by hand cannot show a decode the report does not support.

A METAR written without its keyword is admitted when the time group is followed by a wind group: PHNL 221651Z 07012G18KT is four letters, six digits, a Z and a group ending in KT or MPS, which do not occur together in prose. The keyword form is read anywhere on a line; the bare form is read only at the start of one.

A terminating = stays outside the link, as the // on a USMTF set line stays outside an airfield link. A time group inside a report is never linked on its own: the report is the longer match and wins. When the report around it does not decode, the time group is linked as a date-time group, exactly as it was before this decorator existed.

A report posted on its own becomes a table

When the message is nothing but one report, on one line or several, the message is rewritten into a table of the decode: the one-line summary, then every decoded group, and a last row, Details, whose link opens the panel. The report as you wrote it is not in the table; the panel and the link's page show it verbatim. The header names the station, and when the build holds that airfield the name is a link to its panel. Every time in the table, the issued or effective time and a NOTAM's expiry, is a date-time link, so hovering it gives the reader's zones. The table is markdown in the message itself, exactly as the airfield table is, so the post keeps its search matches, the table is in every export, and it renders on every client whether or not it runs this plugin.

A report inside a longer message gets the link alone, and a message that carries several reports one per line gets a link on each. What the table says is what this build decoded at the moment of posting; the panel decodes the report again every time it opens. Setting: EnableAvReportTable.

A fenced report becomes a card

A report in a fenced code block labeled metar, speci, taf or notam, whether or not the report inside it is one line, is rendered as a card under the message, with the message itself left exactly as written. A fence is never rewritten, and the label is your statement of what it holds. A report on several lines posted on its own without a fence gets the table instead, and the card only while that is turned off.

The card shows the report as posted, the summary line, the decoded groups, each TAF period under its own heading, the remarks, anything not decoded, and the station on a map. Open details opens the same thing in the sidebar.

A card costs the post its search matches.

Rendering a card marks the post with a custom type, which excludes it from Elasticsearch and OpenSearch results and Recent Mentions and drops its link previews and automatic translation, exactly as the Cursor on Target and GeoJSON cards do. A report that became a link or a table is never affected, and stays searchable. Turn EnableAvReportCard off to keep fenced reports as ordinary text.

One report per message. A message carrying several reports one per line gets a link on each line rather than a card, because each line is independently linkable and searchable. A multi-line report pasted inside a longer message is neither linked nor rendered: linking its first line alone would store a truncated report. Put it in a fence, or post it on its own.

The month and year are taken from the post

A METAR's 221651Z is a day, an hour and a minute. The month and year come from the moment the message was posted, exactly as the short date-time group resolves, so a report pasted long after it was issued shows the month it was posted in rather than the month it was issued. A TAF's validity period and a NOTAM's effective times carry their own dates and need no inference.

Deliberately declined

InputWhy
metar phnl 221651z ...Lower case. Reports are upper case on every wire that carries them.
PHNL 221651Z ready for departureNo wind group after the time. The time group is linked as a date-time group instead.
the METAR is lateThe keyword alone is a word. A station and a time group have to follow it.
wx:METAR PHNL ...A colon touching the keyword fails the clean-edge rule. Put a space after the label.
A METAR inside a fenced or inline code spanCode is never rewritten. Label the fence metar to get a card.
A three-line TAF in the middle of a longer messageNeither linked nor rendered; see above.
A report over 2,048 characters or 64 linesLonger than any report a station issues, so refused rather than read.
SNOWTAM, PIREP, SIGMET, AIRMETNot read by this build. A group the vocabulary lacks inside a report that is read is kept verbatim under Not decoded, never guessed at.

What the panel shows

The heading is the kind and the station, with the station's name linked to the airfield panel when the build's database holds it. Under it, the summary line, then one row per decoded group in report order: wind, visibility, runway visual range, present weather, sky, temperature and dew point, altimeter, trends and wind shear for an observation; the base forecast and each FM, TEMPO, BECMG and PROB period for a forecast; the location, keyword, effective and expiry times and the text with its contractions expanded for a NOTAM, plus the Q) line's subject, condition, limits, center and radius for the ICAO form. Remarks are decoded where the vocabulary knows them, and anything else is listed under Not decoded exactly as written. Below the rows, As posted holds the report exactly as it was written, collapsed, with a button to copy it, as the Cursor on Target and GeoJSON panels do; the map comes last, as it does in the airfield panel.

The values are rendered on the server, once, so the hover, the panel, the card, the page and the API cannot disagree about the same report. See the sidebar panel for the shared mechanics.

The map

The station's airfield is drawn when the database holds it. An ICAO NOTAM whose Q) line carries a center and a radius draws that instead, as a circle of that many nautical miles. A station outside the database draws nothing and the panel says nothing about position. The map under a card reads the same map under a post switch the coordinate map does, and the panel map the sidebar one.

Where the vocabulary comes from

The weather groups follow the WMO code tables as the FAA Aviation Weather Handbook reproduces them. The NOTAM contractions are the FAA's own list, and the Q) line's subject and condition codes are the ICAO tables the FAA NOTAM manual carries. Every table is compiled into the plugin, with its provenance recorded beside the data in the repository. A code the tables lack is kept verbatim rather than guessed.
