Date-Time Groups

Every date and time shape the plugin recognizes, what each one resolves to, and the ones it turns down.

Every format on this page can be switched off individually in the System Console. See Admin Settings. To see what your own server does rather than what this page says, run /tactical-fusion example-details, which runs the real matcher and posts what it did. Every example below is one of that command's own rows.

The rule everything follows

A date or time is eligible only if it resolves to a single instant. The link bakes that instant into its query string and the panel counts down to it, so anything needing a timezone invented for it is out. That one rule explains most of the declined list below.

Military date-time group

Setting: EnableDTGMilitary

FormExampleNotes
DDHHMM + zone + MMM + YY 091630ZAUG26 Any zone letter except I and J. The month abbreviation is case insensitive.
DDHHMM + zone + MMM + YYYY 091630ZAUG2026 The same, with a four digit year. The link normalizes it to two.
DDHHMM + Z 091630Z Short form. Literal Z only. Month and year are taken from the date the message was posted, and the panel says so.

More of the same, including the ends of the range:

ExampleNotes
091630ZAUG2026four-digit year, normalized to two in the link
091630ZAUG26two digits always mean 20NN
091630Zthe short form: month and year from the post date
010001ZJAN00the earliest the two-digit year can name

The year is restricted to 2000 to 2099, which is exactly the range two year digits can name without ambiguity. Written with four digits, a year outside it is declined rather than accepted and then rendered wrongly.

091630R is not a date-time group here. The short form accepts only Z, because six digits followed by a single letter is far too easy to hit by accident. Write 091630RAUG26 if you mean Romeo.

Zone letters

The letter after the six digits is the zone the time is written in. The plugin resolves it to UTC before it stores anything, so the link carries one instant no matter which letter you used.

ExampleNotes
091630AAUG26A is UTC+1
091630MAUG26M is UTC+12, the furthest east
091630YAUG26Y is UTC-12, the furthest west
091630RAUG26R is UTC-5
091630Zaug26the month is case-insensitive; the zone letter is not
LettersOffset
ZUTC
A to HUTC+1 to UTC+8
K to MUTC+10 to UTC+12
N to YUTC-1 to UTC-12

Two letters are missing, and both on purpose.

I is skipped in the military alphabet because it reads as a 1. J (Juliet) means the observer's own local time, which would make the resolved instant depend on who is reading it. This design bakes one instant into the link, so a Juliet group is declined rather than guessed at.

RFC 3339 timestamp

Setting: EnableDTGTimestamp

The extended form only, with the hyphens and the colons present:

ExampleNotes
2026-08-09T16:30:00Z

2026-08-09T16:30:00.500Za fraction is parsed and discarded
2026-08-09T20:30:00+04:00the same instant, written in +04:00
2026-08-09T22:00:00+05:30a half-hour offset no zone letter can say
2026-08-09T22:15:00+05:45and a quarter-hour one
2026-08-09T11:30:00-05:00behind UTC

1970-01-01T00:00:00Zthe earliest instant a link may carry
2200-01-01T00:00:00Zand the latest

Seconds are optional and a fractional part is parsed and then discarded, since the panel resolves to the second. An explicit offset is what makes this format eligible at all, and it can say things no zone letter can: half-hour and quarter-hour offsets have no letter.

The DTG: label

Setting: EnableDTGMoniker

Some military formats mark where a time starts with a DTG: label. The label is matched and then dropped, so only the token itself becomes the link text:

ExampleNotes
DTG: 091630ZAUG26consumed, so only the token is linked
DTG:091630ZAUG26spacing optional
DTG : 091630ZAUG26either side of the colon
dtg: 091630ZAUG26case-insensitive
Dtg:2026-08-09T16:30:00Zeither grammar

Spacing either side of the colon is optional and the label itself is case insensitive. The token keeps its own casing rules.

The label vouches for nothing.

A token it marks still has to be a real one. DTG: 091630R is declined exactly as 091630R is. The label also only applies to formats that are themselves switched on, so turning military formats off also stops DTG: 091630ZAUG26 from being recognized.

In a sentence

A token does not have to be alone on the line. It is found in ordinary prose, twice on one line, and beside a different decorator, and what surrounds it is left exactly as written:

ExampleNotes
ARCT 091630ZAUG26 confirmedonly the token is linked
window opens 090600ZAUG26 and closes 091800ZAUG26two on one line
[the plan](https://example.com) says 091630ZAUG26the link is untouched
run `status --now` at 091630ZAUG26the code is untouched
091630ZAUG26 at 21.3353, -157.9483two decorators on one line

The declined list is long, and each entry is declined for a stated reason rather than by oversight. Nothing below is decorated.

Outside the range a link may carry

Instants run from 1970 to 2200. Both ends are enforced identically at decoration and at render, so a token accepted in a message can never fail on its own page.

ExampleNotes
1918-11-11T11:00:00Zbefore 1970
2201-01-01T00:00:00Zafter 2200

What the panel shows

Clicking a date-time link opens the right-hand sidebar. See the sidebar panel for the mechanics that are the same for every decorator.

Three lines, then a table:

Countdown Time remaining until the instant, counting down live: in 3h 20m 15s, 2d 1h 0m 5s ago, or now. It flashes once the instant is close, which by default means within 30 minutes.
Reading The instant written out in full, in the offset it was originally written in: 09 Aug 2026 16:30 Z. Seconds appear only when there are seconds to show, so 2026-08-09T16:30:45Z reads 09 Aug 2026 16:30:45 Z while 2026-08-09T16:30:00Z stays 16:30: a written :00 has nothing to lose, and padding it on would claim a field the row does not need. A date-time group has no seconds field, so it never shows one.
Canonical form The token as the parser understood it, in monospace. Useful for confirming that a short form was read the way you meant, and it is what travels in the link.
Timezone table The same instant in each of your timezones, ordered west to east. Times follow the reading: to the minute, and to the second when the original text was written that finely.

The date column badges

A +1 or -1 next to a date means that row falls on a different calendar day from the token itself. The comparison is against the token's own zone, not UTC, so a group written in +04:00 does not badge half the table as a day out.

The zones are ordered west to east measured at that instant rather than by a fixed offset, so a zone observing daylight saving sits where it actually is on the day in question.

Short forms say what they assumed

A short form such as 091630Z carries no month and no year, so both are taken from the date the message was posted. The panel states this in italics beneath the reading rather than presenting an inferred date as fact. A token that named only its year is treated the same way.

The plugin never pads a field the author did not write without saying that it did.

A zone whose data will not load renders n/a rather than being dropped from the table, so the list of zones stays the same length every time you open it.

The hover card shows the countdown and nothing else. A hover answers "how far off is this" at a glance; everything else is a click away.

You can choose which zones appear and how urgent an instant has to be before it is flagged. See Customize your view.

Times generated when you ask

/tactical-fusion example-details opens with date-time groups built from the moment you ran it, rather than from a fixed date: one a few minutes ahead, one further out, one days out, and one in the past.

That is deliberate. A fixed date cannot show a countdown actually moving, cannot straddle the threshold at which the panel starts flagging, and cannot show the half of the behavior nobody expects until they meet it, which is a past instant counting up.

Settings and troubleshooting

SettingGoverns
EnableDTGEvery format on this page.
EnableDTGMilitaryMilitary date-time groups, long and short.
EnableDTGTimestampRFC 3339 timestamps.
EnableDTGMonikerThe DTG: label, for whichever grammars are on.

Switching a format off does not break existing links.

A format switch governs decoration only. A link already written into a message keeps working after its format is switched off, which is why the decorator stays registered with everything off. Turning a switch off is the safe way to stop new decoration.

If a date you expected to be linked was not, see Troubleshooting.
