The Sidebar Panel

How a hover, a click and a standalone page work, and how to make them your own.

This page covers what every decorator's panel has in common. For what a particular panel shows, see Date-Time Groups, Coordinates, Airfields or Cursor on Target.

Hovering a link

Point at a decorated time and a small card appears showing the countdown only: how long until that instant, or how long since. That is deliberately all it shows. The reading and the timezone table are worth opening the sidebar for; a countdown is worth a glance.

Point at a coordinate and you get the same card with a small map only, showing where it is and the square it names. It is not interactive: the controls and the wheel belong to the panel, which is one click away and carries every reading. See the hover card for the whole of it.

Point at an airfield code and the card names the airfield and where it is, and shows nothing else. A four-letter code says nothing at all on its own, so naming it is exactly what a glance is asking; the readings are one click away.

Clicking a link

Clicking opens the right-hand sidebar. The browser does not navigate, so you do not lose your place in the channel, and the panel follows you as you click other links in the same channel.

Every panel is built the same way: a heading naming what you clicked, the readings that decorator produces, and the controls for hiding rows you do not want. What fills the middle is the only part that differs:

Date-Time Groups A live countdown, the instant written out, the canonical token, and the same instant in each of your timezones.
Coordinates The position in every notation this build knows, each one copyable, with the resolution it was written to and a map.
Airfields The code, the place, the type, the elevation and the IATA code, with the position handed to the coordinate panel.
Cursor on Target Everything the card shows, plus the device, the position quality, the payload, the shape, the processing path, and the event as posted.

Only one thing in the sidebar ticks.

The date-time countdown and the Cursor on Target Goes stale row are counted against your own device's clock and update as you watch. Every other reading in every panel is computed once, on the server, and does not move. A Cursor on Target event that has already gone stale stops counting and says so.

The standalone page

Clients that do not run the plugin's web bundle, notably the mobile app, get a page of their own instead of the sidebar. It shows the same countdown and the same table, because for a coordinate it runs the same code the sidebar does: the server validates the link and works out every reading, and the page draws them.

It requires a Mattermost login. Opening one of these links without a session takes you to the sign-in page first and then on to the coordinate you clicked, so a session that expired while you were reading costs you a sign-in rather than the link. The map page behind Open larger works the same way.

The standalone page always shows the built-in defaults.

The page does not ask who is reading it, even though it now requires a login: it is rendered from the link alone. If you have customized your view, the sidebar and the page will disagree about the same date-time group.

Customize your view

Below the timezone table is a Customize your view link. It replaces the panel rather than expanding underneath it, because its timezone picker runs to several hundred rows and putting that below the table would bury the date-time group you opened the sidebar to read. A Back link returns without saving.

Two things are yours to set:

Timezones Which rows the table shows, up to 25. Chosen from a catalog of named military installations, or from every timezone your browser knows.
Flash threshold How close the instant has to be before the countdown flashes, in minutes. Between 1 and 1440.

Saving closes the editor, because the changed table behind it is the receipt. A save that fails does the opposite and stays open with the reason on screen, since closing would throw away both the message and your edits.

Restore defaults

Restore defaults deletes your settings rather than writing today's defaults into them. The difference matters: a reader who has never chosen keeps tracking whatever the defaults become, instead of being frozen at the list that happened to ship in this version.

For the same reason, a selection that is exactly the defaults collapses back to "nothing chosen" when you save it. Opening the editor and pressing Save does not quietly pin your table forever.

Your settings are per user

They are yours alone and follow you between clients. They are never shown to anyone else and never change what is stored in a message.

Choosing timezones

The picker is a combobox: type to filter, arrow keys to move, Enter to pick, Escape to close. Every term you type has to match, in any order, so berlin spang works. The identifier is searched both as written and with its separators opened out, so both america/los and los angeles find Los Angeles. The offset is searchable too, so +05:45 works.

The list has two groups:

  • Military installations, a curated catalog with names.
  • All timezones, every zone the browser knows, unnamed.

Several installations can share one timezone, and you can pick both.

Somebody at Stuttgart wants a row that says Stuttgart, not the Ramstein row that keeps the same clock. The two rows read identically to the minute, which is the accepted cost of naming both.

Names are never guessed from a timezone. Picking a bare Europe/Berlin out of "All timezones" gives a row reading Berlin, not Ramstein, so it cannot sit next to a real Ramstein row looking identical.

Row order

Rows are always ordered west to east by UTC offset, in the sidebar, the editor and the standalone page alike. The order you add them in carries no meaning and is not stored.

Offsets are measured at the date-time group's own instant, never looked up from a table. Roughly half of these zones observe daylight saving, so a stored offset would be an hour wrong for part of the year, and two rows can swap places between summer and winter. That is correct behavior, not a bug.

A zone whose offset cannot be measured sorts last rather than as UTC. Filing an unknown under Zulu would be a claim rather than an admission.

Nothing here may fail the panel

A settings read that fails, a stored blob that will not parse, and a timezone this browser cannot format each fall back to a default rather than taking the panel down. A save is the exception and reports its error, because a save that quietly did nothing is worse than one that says so. If you see a message when saving, Troubleshooting lists them all.
